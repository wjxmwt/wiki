PROJECT · DESCRIPTION

A Degradation-Monitoring System for Naphthalene and Other Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons

Urbanization and industrial activity keep bringing invisible soil-pollution challenges. Facing the drawbacks of conventional detection methods — high cost, bulky instruments and poor capacity for on-site rapid screening — our team constructed an engineered-bacteria-based biosensor, aiming to provide a lightweight and practical alternative for environmental PAHs detection.

Research Background Our Project
SYNTHETIC BIOLOGY
PAHs Biosensor

Research Background

PAHs are never far from everyday life. Vehicle exhaust, aging asphalt pavement, smoke from open-air barbecues, and straw and coal combustion continuously release these contaminants. After atmospheric deposition, PAHs accumulate in urban parks, roadside green belts, suburban vegetable plots and farmland soils.

The soil we step on in public parks and farmland where vegetables grow may contain accumulated PAHs. Pollutants can transfer from soil into crops and finally enter human bodies through food chains, posing potential health risks upon long-term exposure.

Current Limitations

Conventional chromatographic detection requires heavy equipment, complex sample pretreatment, high costs, and long turnaround times.

Our Motivation

Bridge the gap for accessible, rapid, and low-cost on-site soil screening for local communities and suburban farmland.

Our Project

In this project, we use E. coli as a chassis and reconstruct the NahR-Psal sensing system coupled with an eGFP reporter module. We adopt a co-culture two-strain strategy: one strain converts naphthalene into salicylate via metabolic pathways, which further triggers downstream fluorescence output. PAHs concentration can be reflected by fluorescence intensity.

We tested our system using local soil samples collected in Wuhan, optimized culture conditions and the signal calibration workflow. Our goal is to build a low-cost, user-friendly microbial detection tool, free from heavy lab instruments, and move toward on-site preliminary screening.

Expected Impact

We hope our biosensor can serve as a complementary tool to conventional instruments. It can offer simple screening options for environmental surveys and community soil investigation, helping identify potential polluted sites efficiently. Meanwhile, we wish to demonstrate the practical value of synthetic biology within environmental monitoring.
