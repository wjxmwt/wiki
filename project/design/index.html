PROJECT · DESIGN

Dual-Strain Co-Culture PAHs Biosensing System

Read Introduction View Optimization

Introduction of Design

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) are ubiquitous persistent organic pollutants in urban soil and water environments, mainly derived from vehicle exhaust, asphalt aging, cooking fumes and fossil fuel combustion. As the simplest and most frequently detected low-molecular-weight PAH, naphthalene poses carcinogenic and teratogenic risks under long-term low-dose exposure. According to Chinese national standard GB 36600-2018, strict risk screening values are defined for soil naphthalene contamination. However, conventional HPLC detection requires complicated pretreatment, high cost and laboratory dependence, making rapid on-site screening impossible.

In this project, we constructed a dual-strain co-culture biosensing system based on the native naphthalene degradation pathway and NahR-Psal sensing pathway. The engineered degrader strain metabolizes naphthalene into salicylic acid, while the sensor strain recognizes salicylic acid and triggers eGFP fluorescence expression. The normalized fluorescence intensity (FL/OD₆₀₀) can quantitatively reflect environmental naphthalene concentration.

The wild-type system suffers from low sensitivity, weak signal output and insufficient degradation efficiency. Therefore, we performed systematic optimization from five dimensions: bidirectional promoter engineering, NahR saturation mutagenesis, degradation promoter replacement, culture condition optimization, and dual-strain system adaptation. Our iterative modifications greatly improved sensitivity, response speed and detection limit, realizing a low-cost and high-performance whole-cell biosensor for environmental PAH screening.

Wild-Type System Design & Deficiency Analysis

The original pathway is derived from Pseudomonas putida G7, which contains the native NahR-Psal-Pr bidirectional regulatory pathway and complete naphthalene degradation gene cluster. In wild-type strains, NahR recognizes salicylic acid and activates the Psal promoter to drive reporter expression, while the nah gene cluster catalyzes naphthalene-to-salicylate conversion.

However, after being transferred into E. coli DH5α, the native system showed severe limitations. The wild-type Psal-Pr bidirectional promoter poorly matches the E. coli transcriptional system, resulting in low transcription efficiency. Native NahR exhibits poor sensitivity to low-concentration salicylic acid. The original degradation promoter is weak and limits naphthalene conversion efficiency. The unoptimized system presents high baseline, low signal output and narrow dynamic range, which cannot detect trace environmental naphthalene pollution. Based on these deficiencies, we carried out multi-round rational engineering and system optimization.

Five-Dimension System Optimization

Bidirectional promoter engineering

The wild-type Psal-Pr bidirectional promoter contains atypical ‑10 and ‑35 sequences, leading to low RNA polymerase binding affinity and poor transcriptional activity in E. coli. We performed site-directed mutagenesis on core promoter regions: the ‑10 box was optimized to TATAAT and the ‑35 box to TTGACA, fully matching the conserved consensus sequence of E. coli.

The optimized promoter significantly enhanced transcriptional output. The salicylic acid detection limit decreased from 0.1 mM to 20 μM, achieving a 5-fold improvement in sensitivity, which laid a solid foundation for trace signal capture and low-concentration pollutant detection.

Result: Salicylic‑acid detection limit 0.1 mM → 20 μM (5× more sensitive) after ‑10/‑35 box optimization to TATAAT / TTGACA.

NahR saturation mutagenesis

NahR is the core sensory protein that determines the system's salicylic acid recognition sensitivity. Based on the optimized Psal promoter backbone, we constructed a saturation mutagenesis library targeting key functional sites N169 and R248 of NahR. After plate preliminary screening and liquid rescreening, we obtained the high-performance mutant NahR‑118 (G169T248).

This mutant exhibits obvious fluorescence induction under extremely low salicylic acid concentration (1 μM), greatly improving the system's ultra-low concentration detection capability. In addition, the mutant plasmid presents a relaxed topological conformation, which optimizes NahR‑ligand and NahR‑DNA interaction, amplifies transcriptional output, reduces background noise and improves signal‑to‑noise ratio.

Result: NahR‑118 (G169T248) responds clearly to 1 μM salicylic acid, with reduced background and improved signal‑to‑noise ratio.

Degradation promoter optimization

The wild-type naphthalene degradation gene cluster is weakly expressed in E. coli, resulting in low naphthalene-to-salicylate conversion efficiency and insufficient sensory signal. To strengthen upstream degradation performance, we tested five constitutive promoters with different expression strengths and selected P114 as the optimal replacement for the native promoter.

After promoter replacement, bacterial naphthalene metabolism was significantly enhanced. The coupled degradation-sensing system achieved a 1.4‑fold higher fluorescence output compared with the original system, effectively smoothing the whole signal pathway from naphthalene degradation to fluorescence generation.

Result: Native degradation promoter replaced with P114, giving 1.4× higher coupled fluorescence output.

Culture condition optimization

Naphthalene dioxygenase (NahA), the key degrading enzyme, relies on [2Fe‑2S] metal cofactors for catalytic activity. To further enhance degradation efficiency, we systematically optimized medium composition, metal ion supplementation and incubation time. The results proved that supplementation of 0.5 mg/mL Fe²⁺ in LBA100 medium maximizes enzyme activity.

Under optimal conditions, the system completes naphthalene degradation and produces stable fluorescence signals within only 3 hours, realizing rapid detection and greatly shortening the experimental cycle.

Result: 0.5 mg/mL Fe²⁺ in LBA100 medium maximizes NahA activity; stable fluorescence achieved within 3 hours.

Dual-strain co-culture optimization

Bacterial ratio and cell density are critical to the stability and sensitivity of the co-culture system. We optimized the inoculation ratio and working OD gradiently, and finally determined the optimal working density of sensor strain as OD₆₀₀ = 0.8. The standardized dual-strain system exhibits high stability and repeatability, achieving an ultra-low detection limit of 0.1 μM for naphthalene, which fully meets the detection demand of trace environmental pollutants.

Result: Optimal sensor-strain working density OD₆₀₀ = 0.8; standardized dual-strain system reaches a 0.1 μM naphthalene detection limit.

Design Summary & Future Prospect

To solve the low sensitivity, weak signal and low degradation efficiency of the wild-type system, we performed five-layer iterative optimization: bidirectional promoter modification enhanced transcription, NahR saturation mutagenesis improved ultra-trace recognition, degradation promoter replacement strengthened metabolism, metal ion optimization accelerated catalysis, and dual-strain standardization stabilized signal output.

Finally, we constructed a high-sensitivity, fast-response and high-stability dual-strain PAHs biosensing platform. Free from reliance on large precision instruments, our system enables low-cost and rapid screening of naphthalene pollution in soil and water, providing a novel and convenient synthetic biology solution for urban invisible organic pollution monitoring.

For practical application, we plan to develop portable PAHs detection kits based on our optimized biosensing system, suitable for on-site field detection, community soil surveys and suburban farmland sampling. Furthermore, we have built a supporting online photographic identification platform. By capturing fluorescence images under ultraviolet excitation and utilizing the fitting conversion relationship between green saturation and normalized fluorescence intensity, the platform can intelligently and quantitatively calculate naphthalene concentration, realizing a fully lightweight detection process of "sampling‑culturing‑photographing‑intelligent quantification" and further lowering the threshold of environmental pollutant detection.
