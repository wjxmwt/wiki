Daily Safety in BSL-1 Lab

Safety is the core principle of our project throughout gene cloning, plasmid construction, engineered strain cultivation, dual-strain biosensor assay and environmental soil sample tests.

We use E. coli DH5α as the chassis to construct naphthalene-sensing and naphthalene-degrading engineered strains. Experiments involve organic solvents including naphthalene, acetonitrile and DMSO. All laboratory work is performed in a BSL-1 laboratory. Our work strictly complies with national biosafety regulations and iGEM safety guidelines.

A complete safety management workflow has been established to keep every experimental step safe and controllable.

Biosafety & Protection

Microorganism handling

The chassis E. coli DH5α used in our project belongs to Risk Group 1 and is non-pathogenic. Our sensor and degrader engineered strains are stored as glycerol stocks in dedicated ‑80 °C freezers. Each stock is clearly labeled with strain name, genotype, construction date and responsible member.

Glycerol stock revival is performed inside a laminar flow cabinet. Only small volumes are taken for streaking or inoculation to avoid repeated freeze‑thaw damage.

All liquid cultures are carried out in sealed shake flasks sealed with breathable sterile membranes to guarantee gas exchange and prevent spillage and cross‑contamination. Dual‑strain co‑culture is performed in closed liquid systems to minimize aerosol risk.

Genetic modification & potential risks

We introduced the NahR‑Psal sensory module and naphthalene‑degrading nah gene cluster into E. coli DH5α to build our naphthalene‑responsive dual‑strain system. The original gene fragments come from environmental Pseudomonas putida G7. The engineered strains only gain naphthalene‑sensing and metabolic functions. Nevertheless, unforeseen risks from genetic modification still exist. All cultivation, transfer and sample handling of modified organisms follow BSL‑1 requirements. Release of engineered bacteria outside laboratory premises is strictly forbidden.

Containment strategy

  • Inside laboratory: all open‑tube operations with engineered bacteria are conducted inside laminar flow cabinets. Bench surfaces are disinfected with 75% ethanol after use.
  • Outside laboratory: engineered strains are never taken out of the lab. All liquid waste and solid consumables containing modified bacteria must be fully inactivated before disposal.
  • We do not build dedicated genetic kill‑switches; physical laboratory management serves as our containment measure.

Personal protective equipment

All lab members wear appropriate personal protective equipment. Two levels are defined: basic protection and enhanced protection.

Basic PPE

For routine work such as inoculation, streaking and medium preparation:

  • Lab coat: long‑sleeve splash‑resistant coat, worn only inside laboratory.
  • Safety goggles: protect eyes from splashes; contact lenses are not a substitute.
  • Nitrile gloves: check for damage before use; change gloves between different samples to avoid cross‑contamination.
  • Footwear: closed‑toe shoes; sandals and slippers are prohibited.
  • Long hair must be tied back; necklaces, rings and similar ornaments are not allowed.

Enhanced PPE

For handling high‑risk chemicals including naphthalene, acetonitrile and DMSO:

  • Double‑layer nitrile gloves.
  • Face shield: protect against solvent vapor and liquid splashes.
  • Dust‑resistant mask: required when weighing solid naphthalene or antibiotic powders.

Waste Management

Biological waste disposal

  • Solid biological waste: petri dishes, tips, centrifuge tubes and gloves exposed to engineered bacteria are sealed in biohazard bags and autoclaved at 121 °C for 30 min. Only after complete sterilization can they be discarded as general waste.
  • Liquid biological waste: bacterial waste liquid is mixed with 10% final‑concentration sodium hypochlorite. The mixture stands for at least 60 min for full inactivation before draining into dedicated lab sewage.

Chemical waste disposal

  • Organic waste liquid (naphthalene solution, acetonitrile, DMSO): stored in sealed labeled waste containers and collected regularly by qualified third‑party contractors. Direct discharge into drains is forbidden.
  • Sharps waste: needles and broken glass are placed exclusively in sharps containers.

Equipment Safety

  • Laminar flow cabinet: wipe working surface with 75% ethanol before and after use. UV lamps and HEPA filters are replaced periodically with maintenance records kept.
  • Autoclave: operated only by trained personnel. Loading volume does not exceed 80%. Do not open the lid until pressure and temperature fully return to safe levels.
  • Fume hood: verify proper ventilation before use. Keep the sash lowered during organic‑solvent operations. Leave ventilation running for a period after finishing experiments.
  • Shaking incubator: secure shake flasks firmly to avoid breakage and spillage during high‑speed incubation.

Safety Training & Emergency Preparation

Training & safety review

  • Full‑member safety training: every team member completes BSL‑1 safety training and passes theoretical & practical assessments before lab access. Training covers microbial hazards, chemical risks, waste disposal and emergency response.
  • Regular safety meeting: monthly safety review meetings are held to identify potential hazards and update safety protocols.

Emergency plan

  • Organic solvent spill (naphthalene / DMSO / acetonitrile): maximize ventilation. Absorb the spill with absorbent cotton. Clean with plenty of water and collect residues into the chemical waste container.
  • Skin exposure to chemicals: rinse affected skin with plenty of running water for a minimum of 15 min. In case of eye splashes, use the eyewash station continuously and seek medical support.
  • Bacterial liquid spill: wear gloves. Cover the contaminated area with sodium‑hypochlorite‑containing disinfectant wipes, leave for 30 min for inactivation, then wipe clean.
